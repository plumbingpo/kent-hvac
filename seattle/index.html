Keeping Your Seattle HVAC System Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a system that runs for years and one that breaks down in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the first heat wave of the year.

The earlier you report a comfort problem, the cheaper it usually is. A capacitor that's starting to fail costs little to replace, yet that same capacitor, neglected through a full season, can ruin the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast as soon as something seems off in your home. A quick call saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
