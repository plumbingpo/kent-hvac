When the heat goes out in Kent, WA, the clock starts running, and the diagnosis depends entirely on what kind of system is doing the failing. On a gas furnace, the single most frequent culprit is a flame sensor coated in oxide: the burners light, the sensor cannot prove the flame, and the board shuts the gas down three tries later and locks out. Close behind come cracked hot surface igniters, stuck pressure switches, clogged condensate traps on high-efficiency models, and blower motors that have quietly worn out. Heat pumps fail differently: a weak defrost cycle leaves the outdoor coil armored in ice, a failed reversing valve strands the system in cooling, and a dead outdoor unit leaves the electric backup strips carrying the whole house at several times the operating cost.

When to Call for Heating Repair in Kent

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • No warm air at the registers even with the heat turned up
  • The furnace clicking and trying to light but shutting back down
  • Uneven heat, with certain rooms that never warm up
  • A rotten-egg, burning, or musty smell once the heat kicks on
  • The system starting and stopping every few minutes without warming the house
  • Higher heating bills with less comfort to show for them
  • Booming or banging at ignition, a sign of delayed burner light-off
  • Thick ice or frost covering the heat pump outside

Why Professional Heating Repair Matters

Some of the most dangerous heating failures are the ones nobody notices. A cracked heat exchanger can send combustion gases into the supply air, a blocked or failing flue can spill carbon monoxide into the house, and a furnace that lights late booms gas inside the exchanger, weakening it a little more every cycle. Without instruments, none of it is easy to spot. Even routine symptoms can mislead: a short-cycling furnace may have a bad limit switch, or it may simply be shutting down to protect itself from a choked filter. Clean a flame sensor without asking why it fouled, and the next lockout is already on the calendar.

That is why Kent HVAC does not work on hunches. Our technicians read flame signal on a meter, look at the heat exchanger directly, confirm the flue is drafting, and compare temperature rise to the data plate to make sure the blower speed fits the burner. On heat pumps we check defrost controls, sensors, and the reversing valve before blaming the compressor, and we find out whether the electric backup heat has been running in the background and driving up your costs. Nothing is called fixed until it has run a full cycle and proven itself safe and correct, because that is what the machine heating your home deserves.

Maintenance Tips for Heating Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Never store paints, solvents, or chlorine products near the furnace; their vapors corrode the burners
  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
  • Replace filters monthly through the heating season to keep airflow strong and the heat exchanger from overheating
  • Avoid crowding the furnace with storage, which can starve the burners of air
  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly and put one on every level of the home
  • Have the system serviced each autumn, before the weather tests every weak component
  • Call about booming at startup or new rattles right away, while the fix is still inexpensive
