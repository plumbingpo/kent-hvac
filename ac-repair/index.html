Most AC breakdowns in Kent trace back to a small part: the run capacitor. It helps start the compressor and condenser fan, loses strength slowly over the years, and tends to give out on the hottest afternoon, which is why so many service calls begin with an outdoor unit that hums while the fan sits motionless. Other frequent culprits include contactors with pitted, burned points, fan motors whose bearings have run dry, slow refrigerant leaks that steadily rob capacity, and evaporator coils that turn into blocks of ice once a dirty filter chokes the airflow.

Warm air from the vents can mean a failed capacitor, a refrigerant leak, a frozen coil, or a duct problem, and each one carries a very different price to fix. That is why Kent HVAC tests before touching anything. We meter capacitors against their microfarad rating, compare compressor and fan amp draws to the data plate, judge the refrigerant charge by superheat and subcooling instead of pressure alone, and inspect the coil, blower wheel, and filter whenever there is ice. Adding refrigerant without locating the leak only buys cold air until the charge leaks out again.

Central air, heat pumps running in cooling mode, and ductless mini splits from every major brand are all part of our daily work. Because our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, most AC repairs are completed the same visit. If the compressor has failed on an older system, we tell you so directly and show you the numbers on repairing versus replacing. Signs You Need AC Repair in Kent

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Higher summer bills even though the house still feels warm and humid
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • A hot electrical smell or a tripped breaker whenever the air conditioner kicks on
  • Warm or room-temperature air blowing when the thermostat calls for cooling
  • Puddles near the air handler or water stains on the ceiling beneath it
  • A condenser fan that sits still while the outdoor unit hums
  • Hissing or bubbling sounds that suggest a refrigerant leak
  • An AC that turns on and off every few minutes but never cools down the house

The Benefits of Professional AC Repair

An air conditioner puts several hazards into one cabinet. The capacitor can deliver a painful shock long after the breaker is off, the condensing unit is wired for 240 volts, and the refrigerant circuit is a sealed, pressurized loop that federal rules forbid venting. Servicing it properly takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a DIY recharge can. Diagnosis has its own traps. A frozen coil is as likely to be an airflow issue as a charge issue, and recharging a system that still leaks wastes money while moisture and acid work on the compressor.

What you get from a pro is a repair that holds. Our experienced technicians find leaks with dye and electronic detectors, base every charge decision on superheat and subcooling, test electrical parts with a meter before condemning them, and measure static pressure and inspect the blower and filter any time airflow is in question. Parts-cannon fixes tend to bring you back in a few weeks. Ours are built to last through the heat. And if the numbers say replacement is the better call, we show you those numbers honestly, without pressure.

AC Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Schedule a spring tune-up so weak parts are caught before summer arrives
  • Keep supply and return vents open; closing rooms raises static pressure on the blower
  • Shade helps, but never enclose the outdoor unit; it needs free airflow on all sides
  • Keep shrubs two feet from the outdoor unit and gently rinse its coil each spring
  • Swap filters monthly while the AC is running hard to keep the evaporator coil from icing
  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
  • A new buzz or click at startup often means a weak capacitor, so call before it fails
