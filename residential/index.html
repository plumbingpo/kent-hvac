Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

No homeowner likes surprises on the final bill. So Kent HVAC prices every Kent job before the work begins, after a full diagnostic that looks at the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down gradually, and a weak capacitor lasts right up until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every call is an emergency. Plenty of Kent homeowners call us to size up an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. Seasonal Tune-Ups

Maintenance Plans for Kent Homes

A Kent HVAC maintenance visit is more than a filter change. The technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, replaces the filter, and checks static pressure in the duct system. Small problems get caught while they're still cheap to fix, and your system stays running at full efficiency.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. This is especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's comfort demands, and your local climate.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule cuts operating costs. A clean coil transfers heat far more efficiently, a properly charged system stops overworking the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing an HVAC system even one year early.

Right-Sized Systems

The Right Size System for Your Home

When it comes to HVAC, bigger is not better. An oversized unit turns on and off too often, burns extra energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs nonstop and still falls behind on the hottest afternoons. We don't simply copy the tonnage of the old unit. Instead, we run a proper load calculation using your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

Homes change over time. An addition or a finished basement can outgrow the single-stage system that used to be plenty. Modern two-stage and variable-speed heat pumps hold tighter temperatures and use far less energy. Whether you're comparing a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

The HVAC Repairs Kent Homes Need Most

Homes in Kent run into a familiar set of heating and cooling failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Kent HVAC handles them all: no cooling, air that blows but never gets cold, furnaces that won't ignite or short-cycle, frozen evaporator coils, refrigerant leaks, condensers that hum without starting, thermostats that lose their settings, clogged condensate drains, and mini-splits flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The right repair depends entirely on what actually failed. On cooling systems, that usually means a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor. On furnaces, it tends to be an igniter, flame sensor, or control board. Leaking ductwork pushing conditioned air into the attic is sealed and repaired, and we recover and recharge the refrigerant once the leak itself is repaired. A few of these problems are simply a nuisance, but a cracked heat exchanger or a clogged condensate drain can be a genuine safety or water-damage risk, so we treat it with urgency.
